Valuation Disciplines

Business Valuation

Business valuation is the process of determining the economic value of a whole business or company unit. It is heavily relied upon during mergers and acquisitions (M&A), taxation, and strategic planning.

Key Approaches: The primary methodologies include the Income Approach (such as Discounted Cash Flow), which focuses on future earning potential; the Market Approach, which compares the business to similar companies that have recently sold; and the Asset-Based Approach, which totals up all investments in the enterprise.

Startup Valuation

Valuing an early-stage company is inherently complex because traditional metrics (like EBITDA or historical revenue) often do not exist. Instead, the focus shifts to future potential, market size, and the founding team's capability.

Key Approaches: Investors utilize specialized models such as the Berkus Method (assigning value to key risk-mitigation milestones), the Scorecard Valuation Method (comparing the startup to average funded companies in the region), and the Venture Capital Method, which calculates pre-money valuation based on expected exit multiples.

Share Valuation

Share valuation focuses on calculating the fair and intrinsic value of a company's equity shares. This is vital for private equity investors, Employee Stock Ownership Plans (ESOPs), and regulatory compliance.

Key Approaches: For stable, dividend-paying companies, the Dividend Discount Model (DDM) is frequently applied. Alternatively, metrics like Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratios and Earnings Per Share (EPS) are used to benchmark the share price against industry peers, adjusting for minority discounts or control premiums as necessary.

Brand Valuation

Brand valuation quantifies the financial value of a brand as an intangible asset. Strong brands drive customer loyalty, allow for premium pricing, and provide a massive competitive moat.

Key Approaches: The gold standard is often the Relief from Royalty Method, which calculates what a company would have to pay to license the brand if they didn't own it. Another method is the Premium Profits Method, which isolates the additional profit margin generated specifically by brand recognition compared to generic competitors.

Asset Valuation

Asset valuation is the process of estimating the fair market value of specific assets—both tangible (like real estate, machinery, and inventory) and intangible (like patents, trademarks, and goodwill). It is required for financial reporting, insurance, and liquidation scenarios.

Key Approaches: Valuation metrics range from Book Value (historical cost minus depreciation) to Replacement Cost (the current cost to replace the asset with one of similar utility) and Fair Market Value (what a willing buyer would pay a willing seller in an open market). Accurately valuing intangible assets is critical in the modern economy, where intellectual property often represents a company's largest asset base.
